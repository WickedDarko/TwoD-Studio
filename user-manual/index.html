Complete Operating Guide & 2D/2.5D Animation Reference

This manual documents every workspace panel, stage tool, cutout artwork rule, and animation pipeline in TwoD Studio—an offline 2D and 2.5D skeletal animation suite. Use it as a practical reference for building custom or preset bone rigs, authoring Smart Bone action dials and Vitruvian sub-rigs, sculpting alpha-aware Contour Fit 2.5D meshes and clipping masks, layering non-destructive Glass Plate Shader FX chips, directing perspective walking paths, mixing 2-channel audio, and exporting finished MP4 video or transparent PNG sequences.

Section 1.1 · Workspace Anatomy

Studio Interface Map, Collapsible HUD & Safe Frame Locking

TwoD Studio is organized around a central animation stage surrounded by collapsible panels and an unobstructed stage HUD. Both floating stage bars (View Bar and Tools Bar) feature a leftmost collapse button to tuck into compact corner pills, and automatically fade translucent whenever you touch, pan, zoom, or draw on the canvas.

1.2 When to Use Setup, Animate, and Professional Modes

01 · Setup Mode

Build, Rig & Anchor at Frame 0

Use Setup Mode when spawning characters, adjusting bone lengths, authoring Smart Bone dials, setting Vitruvian sub-rig groups, or fitting sprites to their bind pose. Setup preserves your current frame while keeping the seeker safely locked until unlocked.

02 · Animate Mode

Non-Retroactive Keyframing

Scrub to any frame and pose bones, swap Vitruvian variants, or add FX chips and audio cues. Editing at Frame > 0 automatically anchors Frame 0 at bind pose so earlier animation is never disturbed.

03 · Professional Mode

Camera, Time, Attach & Shader FX

Unlocks four director panels: Camera (keyframeable lens & parallax), Time (non-linear speed curves), Attach (zero-jump dynamic prop parenting), and FX (Glass Plate shaders & blend modes).

1.3 Universal All-in-One Gizmo & Stage Tool Reference

Selection and manipulation are intentionally separated—Select (V) picks bones or sprites safely without dismantling a character, while the Universal Transform Gizmo (T) consolidates all bone and part operations in one stage controller. On desktop builds, a resizable left vertical tool rail also provides draggable, pinnable stage mini-panels for Smart Bone Dials, Vitruvian Switcher, Spline Chains, Clipping Masks, Multiplane Parallax, and Viseme Lip-Sync.

Stage Tool Available Modes Behavior on Stage
Select (V) Setup, Animate, Pro Safely selects any bone or opaque sprite pixel without pulling limbs apart. When a sprite is selected, reveals the sprite Gizmo box (4 corner scale handles, top rotation stalk, interior nudge area, and draggable pivot crosshair). In Setup Mode at Frame 0, dragging the tip handle of a non-prop bone adjusts its length and angle.
Universal Gizmo (T) Setup, Animate, Pro All-in-one bone transform controller supporting Pose (FK / IK), Move Root, Bone Length, and Part Offset in a single unified on-stage widget—with full Undo transaction coalescing on drag release.
FK (Forward Kinematics) Setup, Animate, Pro Rotates a bone directly around its joint pin while child bones follow in a clean arc with angle-unwrapping protection across ±180°.
IK (2-Bone Inverse Kinematics) Animate, Pro Pulls a hand, forearm, foot, or shin directly to a target point on stage. The analytic 2-bone solver respects finite lower-joint limits and automatically preserves natural knee/elbow bend direction.
Move (Translate) Setup, Animate, Pro Translates an entire character rig or prop across the stage at any frame (`0..N`). Dragging any part of a prop moves its root bone directly so artwork and hierarchy stay locked together.
Draw (Freehand Cel) Setup, Animate, Pro Sketches directly on stage. Drawing at Frame 0 creates a persistent global overlay; drawing at Frame > 0 records a keyframe-specific cel (with Copy/Paste and +1F / +3F clone buttons). Selecting Scene (Background) routes strokes to the background layer.
Pan / Zoom All Modes Drag to pan and two-finger pinch to zoom on touch devices; on desktop, use Middle-Mouse, Ctrl+Drag, or Spacebar+Drag to pan and cursor-anchored scroll-wheel to zoom.
Section 2.1 · Asset Preparation

Preparing Character Sprites, Upright Hands & Seamless Cutout Joints

Whether you draw inside TwoD Studio’s Sprite Paint Studio or export layers from Procreate, Photoshop, Krita, or Clip Studio Paint, four preparation rules ensure your character parts auto-fit cleanly and deform without gaps:

Rule 01 · Orientation

Draw Every Part Upright (0°)

Draw heads and torsos upright, arms and legs hanging straight down, hands with the wrist at the top (anchorY ≈ 0.07) and fingers pointing down, and feet flat pointing right. Never pre-rotate PNG limbs sideways—TwoD Studio aligns upright art to every bone automatically.

Rule 02 · Alpha Bounds

32-Bit Transparent PNGs

Export parts as transparent .png files. TwoD Studio scans opaque pixels (SpriteAlphaBounds) and ignores uneven transparent margins, so both cropped limbs and full-canvas exports scale and pivot accurately.

Rule 03 · Joint Caps

Round Overlapping Limb Ends

Avoid flat straight cuts at elbows, knees, shoulders, or ankles. Give overlapping limb ends a rounded semicircular cap so bending joints up to 140° never opens a V-shaped gap.

2.3 .ZIP Character Bundles & Interactive 3-Option Head Mode Prompt

Compress all of a character’s PNG parts into a single .zip archive and import via Library → Characters & ZIPs → Import .ZIP Bundle. TwoD Studio automatically computes opaque pixel bounds (SpriteAlphaBounds) and anatomical joint pivots on import, files parts into anatomical directories, and generates a 1-tap Character Card.

When a character pack includes both a finished painted head (head.png) and a blank head plus facial parts (head_blank.png, eyes, pupils, brows, mouth), spawning it presents three head modes:

Mode 01

Full Rig + Animated Facial Sub-Rig

Uses head_blank.png and attaches independently poseable eye, pupil, brow, and mouth bones beneath the head for expressions and lip-sync.

Mode 02

Full Rig + Static Drawn Head

Uses the finished painted head.png on a single head bone for clean body acting when individual facial bones aren’t needed.

Mode 03

Head & Facial Sub-Rig Only

Spawns a compact portrait/dialogue rig without the body—ideal for close-up acting shots and lip-sync tests.

Section 3.1 · Kinematics & Advanced Rigging

FK, 2-Bone IK Pins, Smart Bone Dials, Sub-Rigs & Spline Chains

Fig 3.1 — Interactive 2-Bone Analytic IK Reach & Spring Follow-Through (Move cursor or touch across stage) Limit-Aware IK Bend Resolution + Hair & Cloak Springs
Kinematics · FK vs. 2-Bone IK

Limit-Aware IK Bend Resolution

Use FK for sweeping arcs and IK to pull hands or feet to exact world targets. Creating an IK Pin (Pin IK in the Bone Inspector) automatically resolves bend direction from asymmetric finite lower-joint limits (or preserves the current evaluated fold) across all 6 preset rig types—locking feet solidly while preserving animated translations and scales.

6-Stage Deterministic Engine

Evaluation Order

Every frame evaluates deterministically across 6 stages: base keyframed/procedural pose → Vitruvian active sub-rig selection → Pre-IK Smart Bone dials → Analytic 2-Bone IK & IK Pins → Post-IK Smart Bone corrective actions & Spline Chains → unwrapped Spring Physics & Inverse-Bind LBS mesh deformation.

3.2 Smart Bone Action Dials & Corrective Sprite Tracks

A Smart Bone Dial lets a single control bone’s rotation smoothly drive corrective bone poses and sprite-track adjustments (numeric neutral-relative offsets/scales/rotations, visibility toggles, and image swaps) across other parts of the rig—evaluated identically on the live Stage, hit-testing, 2.5D mesh rendering, and FrameRenderer export.

1. Create a Dial on a Control Bone

In Setup Mode, select the bone you want to act as the dial (for example, a head-turn, shoulder, or jaw bone). In the Bone Inspector under Smart Bone Action Dials, tap + Dial on [bone].

2. Author Max° (+100%) and Min° (-100%) Corrective Poses

Authoring starts at Max° (+100%): pose the corrective child/neighbor bones or sprite tracks to close joint creases or turn facial features. Then switch to Pose @ Min° (-100%) and author the opposite extreme. A built-in self-guard keeps the control bone out of its own correction.

3. Scrub Test ° & Finish

Drag the Test ° slider across the full range to verify smooth shortest-arc blending, then tap Done. Both Pre-IK and Post-IK Smart Bone actions evaluate automatically whenever the control bone rotates during animation.

3.3 Isolated Sub-Rig Studio & Vitruvian Sub-Rig Swaps

Sub-Rig Studio Modal

Magnified 2.4× Isolated Editing

Launch Sub-Rig Studio from the bottom of the Left Bones panel to isolate any parent bone (such as a head or hand) and its children at 2.4× zoom. Spawn facial sub-rigs, attach sprites from Library or files, and fine-tune tiny joints without touching the rest of the stage.

Vitruvian Groups

Mid-Shot Rig Variant Switching

Group alternate rigged limbs or head setups inside a Vitruvian Group (VitruvianGroup) and keyframe clean switches between them mid-shot—ideal for swapping between front/three-quarter hands, alternate arm rigs, or head turn setups without rebuilding the skeleton.

3.4 Spline Chains, Unwrapped Spring Physics & Multi-Sprite Layering

Spline Chains & Springs

Tails, Hair, Capes & Gravity

Bind multi-bone chains to a Spline Chain Constraint for smooth bendy arcs, or enable Spring Physics in the Bone Inspector (with Stiffness, Damping, and Gravity). Angle deltas unwrap via shortest-arc math so parent rotations past ±180° never cause spring whip.

Multi-Sprite Bone Stacking

Replace Existing vs. Layer On Top

Attaching a new sprite to an occupied bone prompts Replace Existing (costume/expression swap) or Layer On Top—stacking armor, shadows, or rim-lights on the same bone with independent Opacity keyframes, Blend Modes, Z-Order, and 2.5D Meshes.

Section 4.1 · 2.5D Volume & Masking

Alpha-Aware Contour Fit 2.5D Meshes, Clipping Masks & Walking Paths

Open Mesh 2.5D on any sprite to turn flat artwork into a deformable 2.5D triangle mesh. Rigid sprites, deformed 2.5D meshes, and Glass Plate Shader FX blocks composite inside one unified bone resolvedZIndex pass—so meshed hair, back arms, and foreground torsos always layer in exact depth order on both Stage and Export.

Fig 4.1 — Left: Interactive 2.5D Parallax Mesh Lattice (Move cursor) · Right: Perspective Walking Path with Ground Planting Toggle wireframe visibility anytime via the stage Mesh button
1. Alpha-Aware Contour Fit & Locked Grid Density (2×2 – 10×10)

Inside Mesh 2.5D, enable Contour Fit to sample the sprite’s compact 64×64 alpha-occupancy grid and automatically cull triangles whose centroids fall in transparent space—wrapping the mesh closely around your visible art silhouette. Grid density stays locked after creation so accidental taps never wipe your work, and UV-space topology resampling preserves existing vertex edits when intentionally rebuilding density.

2. Branch-Safe Production Auto-Weights & Full Mesh Undo/Redo

Tap Auto-Weights in Weight Paint mode: auto-weighting restricts candidate bones to the attached bone’s ancestor/descendant branch, preventing cross-branch folding or inverted triangles during deep limb bends. All vertex sculpts and weight paint strokes support full Undo/Redo inside the Mesh Studio header.

3. Per-Frame FFD Sculpting, Retroactive Tween Span & Clipping Masks

Use Deform Vertices (FFD) or Soft-Falloff Brush to sculpt head turns and cloth volume at later frames, and tap Tween Span to blend across any custom range (F_start → F) using Additive Blend (preserving intermediate keys) or Direct Morph. For eyes, inner shadows, and cutaways, assign a Clipping Mask (alphaMatte or meshContourPath with maskVisible toggle) directly in the inspector.

4.2 Perspective Walking Paths, Active Path Manager & Multiplane Parallax

Switch the Left Panel from Bones to Trajectory to walk or run a character across roads, hills, or curved perspective paths with zero foot sliding:

1. Frame Your Shot & Sketch Ground-Planted Waypoints

Use 2-finger pinch-zoom/pan or the Pan / Arrange chip to frame your scene first. Sketch a curve with Freehand Path or place waypoints, and enable Plant Feet on Path with slope alignment so soles stay locked to terrain angles.

2. Logarithmic Start & End Frame Rig Scale (0.05× – 6.0×)

Set Start Frame Rig Scale and End Frame Rig Scale (or quick presets 1× → 2×, 1× → 4×, 1× → 0.45×) with Sync Rig Scale enabled so stride length and root scale foreshorten logarithmically with perspective depth. Combine with per-layer parallaxDepth and the Camera Bone for multiplane camera depth.

3. Bake, Resolve Overlaps & Edit Active Paths

Tap Bake Trajectory to Clip. Overlapping paths prompt to Chain After, Overwrite Overlap, or Replace Old Path, and all baked routes stay editable or removable inside the Active Trajectory Paths manager.

4.3 Collapsible Timeline, Cycle Studio & Retroactive Tween Span

Timeline Feature Usage
Collapsible Character Tree & +◆ / ◆× Collapse or expand any Character Rig or limb branch in the left column. Tap +◆ or ◆× on any row to add or clear a bone’s keyframe at the current frame.
Unified 3-Tab Cycle Studio Tab 1 (Browse & Bake) layers built-in or saved cycles onto specific bone scopes (e.g., Walk on legs + Wave on arms); Tab 2 (Active Baked Cycles) edits speed, amplitude, or range in place; Tab 3 (Save Custom Cycle) captures reusable cycles into the Library. Guided walk/run baking keys only vertical bounce on hips.y so moving the character at the end frame smoothly guides the walk across the stage.

Retroactive Tween Span (F0 → F) Available on Bones, Sprites, and 2.5D Meshes to blend scale, translation, or vertex changes across intermediate frames while keeping Frame 0 locked.
Section 5.1 · Non-Destructive Shader FX

Glass Plate Shader FX Chips (11 Effects · 4 Scopes)

Instead of distorting individual limbs in isolation (which tears elbows and knees apart), TwoD Studio composites the target scope onto a shared world-space Glass Plate bounding box with proportional world padding (35% symmetric + 60% bottom melt margin) and warps a deterministic 32×32 UV lattice. IK/FK bones stay 100% live underneath, pausing freezes the exact frame, and exported renders match the stage 1:1.

11 Built-In Shader Effects

Distortion, Glitch & Atmospheric Shaders

Melt (100-frame top-to-bottom progressive wax meltdown with deterministic column-hashed drip phases and floor puddle), Wiggle / Boil, Ripple, Vortex, Glitch (hybrid scanline slices + RGB channel split), Dissolve, Wave Warp, Pixelate, Chromatic Aberration, Hue Shift, and Glow Outline.

FX Chips Track & 4 Scopes

Character, Subtree, Sprite or Full Scene

Tap +FX on the dedicated FX Chips timeline lane (or long-press a character on stage) to drop a duration chip scoped to a Character, Bone Subtree, Sprite, or Full Scene. Drag chip edges to resize duration, animate Start→End easing curves, or save custom presets to your Library.

5.2 Camera Lens Director, Time Remapping & Dynamic Prop Attach

Pro Tab 01 · Camera Lens & Parallax

Keyframeable Camera Bone

In Professional Mode → Camera, tap Ensure Camera Lens Bone to keyframe pans, tracking shots, zooms, and Dutch tilts, and toggle View Through Lens to preview the exact exported frame.

Pro Tab 02 · Time Remapping

Bullet-Time & Non-Linear Speed Curves

Apply Bullet-Time Mid, Fast-In Slow-Out, Slow-In Fast-Out, Heartbeat Pulse, Ping-Pong, or custom Bezier speed curves across poses, sprites, 2.5D meshes, cels, and FX chips—rendered identically in both live playback and export.

Pro Tab 03 · Dynamic Attach

Zero-Jump Prop Hand-Offs

Switch a prop’s parent bone at any frame (e.g., Scene → Hand R → Scene) with automatic inverse-matrix world compensation so the prop never teleports, plus independent inheritRotation and inheritScale toggles.

Pro Tab 04 · Blend Modes & Locks

12 Blend Modes, Vignette & Stage Safety

Assign per-sprite and background blend modes (Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Color Dodge, Difference, and more), dial in atmospheric vignettes, and lock completed Backgrounds, Character Rigs, Props, or individual Bones in the Outliner to prevent accidental edits.

5.3 Sprite Paint Studio, Smart Lasso & Blank-Canvas Drawing

Tap Paint in the top bar (or select a sprite and tap Edit / Paint Sprite) to open the GPU-accelerated Sprite Paint Studio with multi-layer compositing, Ink Pen, Soft Brush, Airbrush, Marker, Wash, Smudge, mirror/quad symmetry, and blank-canvas prop/asset creation:

Smart Cutout & Transforms

Smart Lasso, Flip H/V & 90° Rotation

Trace unwanted background pixels with Smart Lasso and choose Erase Inside, Keep Inside, or color-aware Smart Cutout. Use Flip H/V and aspect-preserving Rotate 90° CW/CCW (automatically swapping logical stage dimensions without stretching narrow limbs).

Non-Retroactive Cel & Sprite Edits

Keyframe Paint-Overs & Cel Conversion

Editing a sprite at Frame > 0 with Keyframe Mode swaps the sprite image from that frame onward without altering earlier frames. Freehand stage cels can also be long-pressed and rasterized directly onto any Character or Prop bone with zero-jump matrix compensation.

5.4 2-Channel Multi-Track Audio Engine & Viseme Lip-Sync

Audio Engine · BGM & VOX Lanes

Multi-Track Mixer & Native AAC Export

Tap + Audio in the timeline toolbar to load .wav, .mp3, .m4a, or .ogg files (or synthesize studio cues) onto independent Background (BGM) and Voice-Over / SFX (VOX) lanes. Pin or scroll the collapsible Audio group, adjust Start Frame, Trim In/Out, Volume (0–200%), Mute, and Fade-In / Fade-Out; MP4 export mixes both lanes to 44.1 kHz stereo 128 kbps AAC automatically.

Dialogue · Visemes & Lip-Sync

Viseme Bank & Rough-Draft Lip-Sync

Switch mouth shapes rapidly via the Viseme Switcher Bank or generate an automatic rough-draft mouth animation pass from your imported voice-over track, then refine individual dialogue keyframes on the timeline.
